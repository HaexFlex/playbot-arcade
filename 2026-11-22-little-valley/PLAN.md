# Little Valley: plan

Playbot's second flagship (after Lantern Keeper 1.0). A multi-day project that keeps growing.

## Vision

A tiny Alpine valley in your browser that feels alive. You shape the land with your finger
(raise, dig, smooth), make it rain or place springs, and water does what water does: it
pools into lakes, spills over ridges, carves gullies and drops sand where it slows down.
Over many days the valley should grow from a terrain toy into a little living world:
meadows and forests that follow the water, a village whose cottages need a well but hate
floods, day and night, seasons with snowmelt in spring, gentle sound, and an optional
goal mode with small "valley wishes" to fulfil. Always: one self-contained `index.html`,
seeded worlds you can share by name ("Daisy Glacier 5"), smooth on a phone or tablet.

Design rules: calm and cosy, no fail states in sandbox mode; real simulation over fakes;
every feature must be visible on screen (no hidden numbers); stays at 30+ fps at 160² on a phone.

### v3: the little village
- [x] Shower fix (part 1, 2026-11-22): a 14 s Shower on a fresh world no longer wipes out the forest (before: 3929 → 745 trees on "Sunny Larch 37" at 160²; now 4356 → 4488). Water memory in sim seconds: `flood` counts only **standing** water (deep and slow), holds while water flows past and fades fast on dry ground (meadows drown after 30 s, trees after 60 s of standing water); new `rush` field counts a fast deep river, which scours only after 60 s. A lasting lake still drowns (tests). Side effect: the fresh world itself no longer loses ~10% of its trees to the start-up wetting
- [x] Place cottages (part 1, 2026-11-22): Cottage tool (key 8, tap on land; not into water, on steep rock, up in the snow or at the edge; no stacking). Mood: happy with open water within a short walk and dry ground, sad when dry and far from water (or soggy with runoff), flooded while water stands on it. Drawn as tiny houses in 3D (cream walls, sun-lit gable roof, chimney, window that glows when happy, blue water up the walls when flooded) with mood badges (heart / thirsty drop / waves) and chimney smoke, and as house icons on the map. Stats pill: cottage count and happy count, plus ruins (fits on phones: "🏠 3 (2 😊)")
- [x] Floods knock out cottages: a cottage under standing water for 20 sim seconds becomes a ruin (low broken walls); placing a cottage on the ruin rebuilds it (`screenshot-village.png`)
- [x] Fields and paths (part 2, 2026-11-22): sim fields `field`, `ripe`, `path`, `tend`. Each happy cottage tends flat, dry, fertile lowland within a short walk (radius N·0.05, not on paths, slowly clearing young forest): fields grow in about 20 sim s in a patchwork of small square plots (plot size N/56; crop per plot from its coordinates: wheat, barley, rapeseed, cabbage; some plots stay meadow as hedges and gaps) and ripen from brown soil through green to their ripe colour over 120 sim s, are harvested and sown again, each plot at its own point in the season. Untended fields (the cottage turned sad, dry or flooded, or became a ruin) go fallow: weedy straw colour, gone in about 90 s. Standing water for 3 s, deep water or a lasting river wipes a field. Footpaths: every 4th life step each cottage treads a straight path to its two nearest neighbours (within N·0.1, fords allowed) and down to the nearest water (stops at the shore); happy cottages tread faster, paths skip steep rock, fade in ~7 min unused and wash out under water. Drawn into the colour map (rows alternate darker in a checkerboard of directions), so they show in the 3D view and on the map; the colour pass costs about +0.1–0.3 ms (within noise) in headless Chrome, `villageStep` about 0.03 ms per life step. Desktop pill adds "· N fields"
- [x] The village grows by itself (part 2, 2026-11-22): a cottage that has been happy for 200 sim s (time-lapse counts up to 3x) and is not crowded (fewer than 4 cottages within N·0.07) builds a neighbour on a ring 0.024–0.04·N away. Spots are deterministic (seed + cottage index + try count, 16 per try) and must be dry, by the water (the new cottage would be happy), at least N·0.025 from any cottage, and pass the normal Cottage checks. Capped: 24 grown cottages per valley (`P.growMax`), 2 per parent, 80 in total. Hint "The village grows: a new cottage by the water!". A village creeps along a good river bank; on "Daisy Glacier 5" five shore cottages grow to about 9–12 in 10–28 s of fast-forward (`screenshot-village.png`; a map shot goes to `/tmp/lv-village-map.png` to keep the site lean)
- [x] Wells (part 3, 2026-11-22): Well tool (key 9, tap a cottage). A well reaches groundwater (`wellYield` = max(lowland groundwater, ground moisture), so ground soaked by water recently helps): plenty in the lowland, little up the slopes, none on the high rock (h/H > 0.5, `WELL_HIGH`); needs at least `WELL_MIN` 0.12. A cottage with a working well is happy far from any lake or stream, tends fields, and its village grows (new cottages away from the water dig their own well, deterministic). A well runs dry if the ground under it is raised onto the rock. Messages: dug / already has one / too little groundwater / solid rock / tap a cottage. Drawn as a little stone well (brick ring, water inside, posts, crank, red roof; brown when dry) in front of the house in 3D and as a stone ring with a blue dot on the map
- [x] Mill wheels (part 3, 2026-11-22): a cottage within N·0.03 of a fast river cell (deeper than 0.25, faster than 1: a normal valley river, not a still lake) that keeps running for 30 sim s (`c.riverT`, which does not count while it rains, so a Shower's runoff never builds wheels, and forgets 2 s per s without the river) builds a wooden water wheel in the nearest such cell. The wheel turns with the flow at its spot (`c.turn`, while faster than 0.8; drawn as a spoked wheel seen at an angle with paddles and foam, angle advances with flow speed and game speed) and stops when the river dries up (it stays, still); a flood that ruins the cottage takes the wheel. A turning mill grinds: its cottage's fields ripen 1.5x faster (`MILL_RIPEN`). Stats pill: phone "🏠 5 (5 😊) ⚙2", landscape "· ⚙ 2", desktop "· 2 mills". Map: a little turning wheel in the river
- [x] Flour sacks (2026-11-23): a mill cottage (not flooded) gets plump burlap sacks by its door (east of the house: lit west side, a shadowed seam, a tied neck with a little flour), one per `SACK_T` 15 s of grinding (sim time: the wheel turning, not paused, scaled by the speed), up to `SACK_MAX` 3 (two on the ground, one on top), and they stay when the wheel stops. App-side only (`flourT` WeakMap, not saved; a mill that is turning when first drawn starts with one sack). Test hooks `st.sacksDrawn/sackMills/sackFull/sackPos`.
- [x] Flour cart (2026-11-23): when a full pile of 3 sacks has stood `CART_LOAD` 6 s more of grinding, the miller loads them onto a little two-wheeled handcart (plank box, a turning spoke, shafts, three sacks on top) and pulls it, walking in front in a floury white smock and cap, to the nearest standing, unflooded cottage within `CART_MAX` 42 cells along a dry road (no cell deeper than `CART_WET` 0.03 on the way, the first and last 1.5 cells excepted), at `CART_V` 1.6 cells per second of sim time (paused = still). It unloads there with a puff of flour, waits 2.5 s and walks back with the empty cart. If the target floods or falls to ruin on the way it turns round with the sacks. App-side only (`carts` Map, not saved; a grid resample or new valley drops it). Test hooks `st.cartsSent/cartsDrawn/cartsMoving/cartPos/flourDelivered`, `__lv.cartTarget`.

### v4: time and weather
- [x] Day/night cycle (part 1, 2026-11-22): a sim clock (`LV.clockHour`, from `S.time`, so deterministic and faster at higher speeds), one day = `P.dayLen` 720 sim s (~100 s at 1x), starts at 8:00. The sunlit day (6–20 h) takes 70% of it, evening 10%, the **night only 10%** and dawn 10%. The sun crosses the sky on an arc (rises left, sets right) and the terrain light direction follows it (low warm light at the ends of the day). Golden evening and dawn tint, sky gradient day → warm → night, stars and a moon, far mountains, clouds and birds follow the light (birds go home at night). Night is a moonlit blue at most 46% over the valley (test: night luma 62% of noon, bluer), so it stays readable and playable. Happy cottages light their windows from about 18:40 to 6:30 (flickering warm glow added on top; on the map a warm dot). The tint is two `source-atop` fills on the offscreen 3D buffer, so it costs almost nothing. Clock in the title pill ("☀️ 14:20", 🌇, 🌙, 🌧); hints "Evening: the cottage windows light up" and "Good morning, valley!"
- [x] Natural rain (part 1, 2026-11-22), `P.weather` (on in the app, off by default in the sim so old tests stay exact): rain cloud k is planned from the seed (`LV.planRain`: gap 90–210 s before the first, then 220–520 s; 90–170 s long; wind mostly from the west, sometimes from the east; track, radius 0.26–0.38 of the map, strength), so the weather is deterministic per seed. The cloud drifts across the valley and rains under it (soft disc, more on the mountains), about half a Shower's rate at its heart and only there. It swells the rivers (test: flow x1.5 during a rain, +616 water vs a Shower's 59985 over the same time) and the forest is fine. No extra rain while a Shower pours. Drawn as a grey cloud bank with a rain curtain and falling drops, darker ground in its shadow, a greyer sky and faster clouds; on the map a grey disc with drops. Hint "A gentle rain drifts in from the west, feeding the streams". The weather plan and the clock survive a grid resample
- [x] Seasons (part 2, 2026-11-22), `P.seasons` (on in the app, off by default in the sim so old tests stay exact): a year of `P.yearDays` 4 days (one per season, ~400 s at 1x), starting in early spring; `LV.yearFrac`, `LV.season`, `S.yearOff` shifts the season. A **snow line** (`LV.snowLine`, share of the mountain height) sits at 0.64 in summer, comes down to 0.40 in late autumn and creeps to 0.14 in deep winter, then climbs back in spring. Snow is a real sim layer `S.snow` (water height units, `snowStep`, one cheap pass every 6 steps; perf test 1.81 ms/step at 160², 2.63 at 192²): it falls above the line from late autumn through winter (up to 0.8), natural rain falls as snow there, and below the line (or under water) it melts into water, so the spring melt swells the rivers (test: river peak x1.34 vs a valley without seasons; water + snow conserved exactly). High snow slowly melts in summer, so the alp does not pile up year after year. **Drier summer**: rains planned in summer come after 2.2x longer gaps and 20% softer (825 s vs 452 s mean gap in the test). Drawn: snow whitens the colour map (3D and map), pines get snowy branches, flowers hide under snow, leafy crowns turn orange/red/gold in autumn and bare twigs in winter. Season emoji in the title pill (🌸 🌻 🍂 ❄️) and season hints. Resample keeps the snow and the season (`screenshot-autumn.png`, `screenshot-winter.png`)
- [x] Weather events (part 3, 2026-11-22; finished in a takeover), `P.events` (on in the app, off by default in the sim so old tests stay exact): **storms**: now and then (22% of rains, 1.5x as often in summer; never the first rain and never in deep winter) a planned rain becomes a storm (`eventRain`, own random stream per rain so the normal rains of a seed stay the same): a bigger, faster cloud that pours 2.8x as hard (test: river peak x2.11 vs the same gentle rain, more low cells flooded), drawn darker with more, wind-slanted drops, extra cloud puffs, quick lightning flashes with a jagged bolt; ⛈ in the clock, hint "A storm rolls in ...! Heavy rain: low cottages may flood", "· storm" in the desktop pill. **Droughts** (`droughtOf`/`droughtAt`, 60% of years, in mid summer, 10–18% of the year long, ramps in and out): no rain while it lasts (a rain planned inside it waits until it breaks), evaporation up to 3x, the ground dries (less groundwater, slower spread; meadows yellow, flowers fade, trees only rest), and cottages need deeper water nearby, so cottages without a well grow thirsty while those with a well stay happy (test: happy without well 74 → 37, with a well 141 of 161). Hazy sun-bleached light, 🌵 in the clock, hints at start and end, "· drought" in the desktop pill. **Rainbows**: when a rain passes while the sun is out, a soft rainbow opposite the sun for 16 s ("A rainbow over the valley!"). `screenshot-storm.png`
- [x] Frozen lakes and skaters (part 4, 2026-11-22): a real ice layer `S.ice` 0..1 (`iceStep`, in the snow pass every 6 steps, only with seasons). From late autumn still water up at the snow line freezes, and from early winter (y 0.74) all calm water in the valley: shallow water first, while fast rivers (speed above ~0.6–1.2) stay open and running water eats the ice from below. An iced cell lets only 1% of its outflow through (`ICE_HOLD` 0.99) and does not evaporate, so a frozen lake keeps its level all winter (test: 157 → 158 under the ice vs 124 for open water) and the stream piles up behind it; in spring the ice thaws (1/60 per sim s) and the lakes flow again. No water is made or lost (closed-box test, drift 0). Drawn as pale blue-white ice with dark cracks and powdery patches, no ripples (3D and map, lightly smoothed between cells). By day, one or two little **skaters** (red, blue, green or yellow coats) glide in loops on solid ice near each cottage (up to 6 spots, looked up every 2 s). Hints "Frost: the still lakes freeze over (the rivers keep flowing)" and "The ice breaks up: the lakes flow again". `screenshot-ice.png`
- [x] Fireflies and lanterns (part 5, 2026-11-22; finished in a takeover): every well gets a little lantern hanging from its crank beam and every mill wheel a lantern on a post at the bank. From the evening on they glow warm and flicker (bigger and softer than a window), in 3D and on the map, and are dark by day. On spring and summer evenings green-gold fireflies blink and drift over the meadows (about 30 of 70 lit at a time); none in winter or by day. `screenshot-fireflies.png`
- [x] Snowmen (2026-11-22): when snow lies around a cottage (max snow in its 3x3 cells above 0.04), about 3 cottages in 5 (picked by place, so it is stable) have a little snowman in the garden: two snowballs, stick arms, coal eyes, a carrot nose, a red scarf and a dark hat so it reads on white snow. They melt away with the snow (`st.snowmenDrawn`; browser test: 3-14 in winter, none in summer)
- [x] Calendar strip (2026-11-23): a small pill under the clock (`#cal`, placed by `placeCal()` every 0.5 s and on resize) with the year and a 4-season bar (spring green, summer yellow, autumn orange, winter pale blue) whose dark marker sits at `yearFrac`. Desktop and landscape say "Year 2 · mid spring" (early/mid/late thirds of the season, or "day d of spring" if `P.yearDays` > 4), a narrow phone just "Y2". Desktop: right under the title, beside the Share button; landscape: under the clock; narrow phone: under the stats pill, right-aligned. The hint moved down to stay clear of it (desktop 80 px, landscape 66 px, so it clears the strip even while it fades out, phone 124 px). `LV.yearNum(S)` gives the year (from 1)
- [x] Ice-jam flood (2026-11-23), `P.iceJam` (default on, only with seasons): in the cold the biggest ice cover is remembered (`S.icePeak`, cells over 0.5). After a real winter (`JAM_MIN` 40 cells) the ice holds through the late-winter thaw (y 0.97 to `JAM_Y` 0.02; only fast water eats it) while the streams and the first melt pile up behind it, then the whole cover breaks up in one go (`iceJam`: ice → 0) and the held water rushes down the outlet as a short flood wave. A thin winter's ice fades as before. `S.jam` records the break-up (time, cells, water held), saved with the valley; the app shows "The ice breaks up! A spring flood rushes down the river" (not for an old break-up from a loaded save). No water is made or lost
- [x] Break-up floes, rumble and valley birthdays (2026-11-23): at the break-up `iceJam` leaves up to `FLOE_PTS` (48) evenly spaced iced cells in `S.jamPts` (deterministic, not saved, no water moved); the app turns them into pale ice plates with a soft shadow that ride the current (`S.u`, `S.v`), turn slowly, melt within 15–21 s (three times faster when stranded on a bank) and hide behind ridges in the tilted view. The break-up plays one sound (`sndBreakup`, fx bus so mute and volume apply): sharp ice cracks over a deep falling rumble, panned to where the ice was. When the calendar rolls into a new year by itself, a birthday toast pops up in the wish-toast style ("Happy birthday, valley! 🎂 Daisy Glacier 5 turns 1 · 🌱 Year 2 begins") with snowflake sparkles and the chime; a new valley, a loaded save or a calendar jump never counts. Tests: node (floe spots) and browser (floes drawn and drifting and melting, one rumble, the toast on screen, no toast for a jump) on all three sizes
