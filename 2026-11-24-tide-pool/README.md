# Tide Pool

A living rock pool, seen from above. The tide rises and falls over about three minutes. Sand, pebbles, and seaweed stay put; everything else is busy.

**Play:** open `index.html` (one file, no network, works on a phone).

## How to play
- **Tap the water** to send a ripple. The gobies scatter and the anemones shut for a moment.
- **Drag a loose rock** aside. Sometimes a shanny or a porcelain crab was living underneath.
- **Shell** then tap the sand to leave a roomy shell. A hermit crab will walk over if it is bigger than the one it is wearing.
- **Look** names whatever you tap, with a one-line fact.
- **Journal** is the list of species you have found on this device. Gulls, moon jellies, and the animals under rocks start out missing.

## What lives here
Hermit crabs shop for bigger empty shells and swap when they find one. Periwinkles graze the sea lettuce (it grows back in the light). Sand gobies school. Cushion stars crawl. Glass shrimp dart and freeze. Beadlet anemones open underwater and close when the pool dries or you tap nearby. Acorn barnacles sweep with their feet while they are covered.

At low tide the shallows dry, anemones close, and the walkers tuck under rocks. A herring gull may land and poke about. At high tide a wave washes across and can leave a shell, a crab, or a moon jelly.

## Sound
Off until you tap **Sound**. Then you get a soft lap of water, a rush when the high-tide wave comes in, a gull call, and a little plink for each ripple. All of it is made in the browser. Keys: M sound, L look, S shell, J journal, Esc closes the journal.
