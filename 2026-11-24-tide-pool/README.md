# Tide Pool

A rock pool on a shore, seen from above through clear water. The light moves on the surface. Under it, a little crowd goes on with the day.

One self-contained `index.html`. It works offline, on a phone or a computer. Nothing is loaded from the network.

## Play

- Tap or drag the water to stir it. Ripples follow your finger. An anemone you touch closes, and the little fish dart under the ledges.
- Tap a grey stone to turn it over. A shore crab, a sand hopper, or a spare shell may be waiting there.
- **Feed** (F), then tap the pool, to drop a pinch of food.
- **Journal** (J) lists the species you have found. It is kept on this device.
- **Pause** (Space). **Sound** (M) is quiet until the first tap, and the choice is remembered.
- The moon button (N) follows the pool's own clock, or stays day, or stays night.

## The pool

A tide comes and goes about every three minutes. At the top of the tide a wave washes in food, an empty shell, and a creature or two if the pool is short of them. At low tide the water shrinks back to the deep middle. On a warm low tide the rock heats up, snails clamp down, and the shrimp slip under stones.

The pool keeps its own short day. At night, stirring the water wakes a few pale sparks.

Under the water:

- Beadlet anemones shut when they are touched, or when the pool goes warm and shallow.
- Hermit crabs walk the bottom and trade up when they meet a roomier shell.
- Periwinkles graze the green film on the rock.
- Rock gobies school, and hide under the ledges when the tide falls or the water is stirred.
- A starfish creeps toward the mussels.
- Shrimp flick through the open water and vanish into cracks at low tide.
- Limpets stay put. Shore crabs and sand hoppers mostly stay under stones.

The pool has only so much room. High tide brings back anyone who has gone, and nobody multiplies without end.

Soft water, a wave, and little bubbles are made in the browser with WebAudio. There are no sound files.
